What is the quantum rule?

An analyzer measures the spin component along its chosen axis in the x–z plane. Angle 0° is +z, 90° is +x, 180° is −z, and 270° is −x. An atom exiting a channel is then prepared in that channel’s spin state.

For two pure spin-½ states separated by angle Δθ, the chance to obtain the plus outcome is:

P(+) = cos²(Δθ/2)  ·  P(−) = sin²(Δθ/2)

Unpolarized atoms have a 50% chance of either outcome at any angle. Detector fractions fluctuate statistically around these predicted probabilities. The displayed +/− beam paths are schematic: they label spin measurement outcomes, not absolute directions of magnetic force.

Try these experiments

Repeated measurements along the same axis should be predictable. Insert an orthogonal-axis measurement between two z measurements, and the earlier certainty disappears.

Both presets prepare +z atoms and pass only the + outcome between analyzers. Try firing 1,000 atoms to compare the final detector probabilities.

An educational simulation of ideal spin-½ projective measurements; it does not model detailed magnetic-field geometry, atomic trajectories, or imperfect real-world detectors.
